# Hadamard matrix of order 3820

3820 = 4 · 955, with 955 = 5 · 191.

## Construction

Three steps, each checked exactly by `verify/verify.py`.

**1. A quaternary conference matrix of order 478.** The zero-diagonal Szöllősi array of [6796](../6796), with a
border of characters as in [complex-hadamard/TBORDERED.md](../../complex-hadamard/TBORDERED.md), over the group

```
G = ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ × ℤ₂₉,   |G| = m = 232 = 239 − 7,
```

with K₀ = 0 × ℤ₂₉, the quotient map φ: G → Q = ℤ₂³ and T the seven nontrivial characters of Q. A and B map G to
{0, ±1} and are symmetric, with A(0) = B(0) = 0 and ±1 elsewhere; C and D map G to {±1}. The condition is

```
PAF_A(g) + PAF_B(g) + PAF_C(g) + PAF_D(g) = −28   for g ∈ K₀, g ≠ 0,
PAF_A(g) + PAF_B(g) + PAF_C(g) + PAF_D(g) =  +4   for g ∉ K₀,
```

that is −Γ(φ(g)) with Γ = 4 Σ_{χ∈T} χ, as in Theorem 7 of Egan, Kotsireas, London and Ó Catháin with a subgroup of
index 8.
- *Core.* With ω = (1 + i)/2, X_f[g, h] = f(h − g) and R[g, −g] = 1, X = ω X_A + ω̄ X_B and Y = ω X_C + ω̄ X_D R give
  the core Kc = [[X, Y], [Y*, −X*]] of order 464.
- *Border.* It has 14 rows and columns and is constant on the cosets of K₀: quaternary matrices R̂ (14 × 16) and
  Ĉ (16 × 14) whose rows and columns lie in the span of the characters in T, and Xb = −R̂ Mc* Ĉ/16, where Mc is the
  same array over Q built from the coset sums.
- *Result.* Xb is a quaternary conference matrix of order 14, and K = [[Xb, R], [R′, Kc]] of order 478 has zero
  diagonal, entries 1, −1, i, −i elsewhere, and K K* = 477 I.

The proof is that of TBORDERED.md with zero diagonals; it is written out in the docstring of
`_szollosi_zero_diagonal_core` in `verify/verify.py`, which checks every condition.

The sequences are constant on the orbits of the group H of order 7 generated by

```
σ(e, z) = (τ e, 16 z),   τ = [[0, 0, 1], [1, 0, 1], [0, 1, 0]]   on ℤ₂³,   z ∈ ℤ₂₉.
```

τ is the companion matrix of x³ + x + 1, a Singer cycle that permutes the seven nonzero cosets of K₀, and 16 has order
7 modulo 29. −1 ∉ H. C and D are constant on the 34 orbits of H, A and B on the 17 orbits of ⟨H, −1⟩, and the
condition reduces to 18 equations.

| | A | B | C | D |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| row sum | 21 | 21 | 6 | 6 |
| sum on K₀ | 0 | 0 | −1 | −1 |
| sum on each of the seven other cosets | 3 | 3 | 1 | 1 |

21² + 21² + 6² + 6² = 954 = 4 · 239 − 2.

**2. A quaternary Hadamard matrix of order 1910.** q = 2 · 478 − 3 = 953 is a prime ≡ 1 (mod 8). The complex
Yamada–Kiyasu template of [6796](../6796) (Yamada's circulants of order 477 with r = 3, ξ = (1, 2)) turns the
conference matrix into a quaternary Hadamard matrix K of order 2(q + 2) = 1910 with K K* = 1910 I
([complex-hadamard/C1910.json](../../complex-hadamard/C1910.json)).

**3. Turyn's doubling.** H = [[K_r + K_i, K_r − K_i], [−(K_r − K_i), K_r + K_i]] is a Hadamard matrix of order 3820.

## Search

**The structure.** The Singer cycle on Q forces the coset sums to be constant on the seven nonzero cosets, the same
mechanism as in [3356](../3356). With 29 ≡ 1 (mod 7), a multiplier of order 7 on ℤ₂₉ can be paired with it. Only 34
orbits remain per block, and the odd factor keeps g and −g apart.

**The search** ([search/tbcore](../../search/tbcore/README.md); [search/NOTES.md](../../search/NOTES.md) §103) is an
exact compression tower.

- **Q level.** Of the two row-sum patterns, only (21, 21, 6, 6) has a border invariant under τ. Each pattern has
  exactly one tuple of coset sums.
- **ℤ₂₉ level.** There are 3316 feasible tuples, with total weight 10^22.1.
- **Join.** In the fibre of the tuple used, A and B were enumerated completely (882 each). For C and D, 4 · 10⁵ vectors
  were sampled from fibres of 5.9 · 10⁶ and 3.0 · 10⁶. An exact join on all the class equations then covered
  1.2 · 10¹⁷ combinations and gave two solutions in about four minutes.
- **Border.** A search over borders invariant under τ gave 28,812 seed quadruples; the certificate uses the first.

A Gaussian estimate conditioned on the coset sums predicts about 10^5.3 solutions for this pattern, and the hit rate
of the join matched it to within 10 %. Both solutions give Hadamard matrices of order 3820. The certificate holds one
of them; its C and D are stored reflected (C(−x), D(−x)), which is how the program assembled the matrix.

## Status

**New.** The entry 955(3) is unresolved in Cati–Pasechnik Table 4 (v2). Egan, Kotsireas, London and Ó Catháin list
4 · 955 among the open orders (Table 5, 19 October 2026), and the Hadamard Matrix Explorer (math74.com, version 53) has
3820 on its list of unknown orders. Table 4 already knows 8 · 955 = 7640 (Paley I, 7639 is prime), so doubling
settles nothing further. None of the standard recursive constructions applies:

- Paley: 3819 = 3 · 19 · 67 and 1909 = 23 · 83 are not prime powers.
- Miyamoto: 955 ≡ 3 (mod 4).
- Yamada: it needs a symmetric conference matrix of order 478. 477 = 21² + 6² allows one, but none is known
  (srg(477, 238, 118, 119) is open in Brouwer's table).
- Kiyasu: q = 953 ≡ 1 (mod 8), not 5.
- Cooper–Wallis: it needs Williamson-type matrices of order 191 ([FRONTIER.md](../../FRONTIER.md)).
- Product theorems: 3820 ≡ 4 (mod 8).

**Ingredients.**
- The quaternary conference matrix of order 478 is new to our knowledge. Paley-type constructions need 477 = 3² · 53
  to be a prime power. Fallon and Iverson's numerical conference signal on ℤ₄₇₇ (arXiv:2610.09169) is not equivalent
  to a quaternary matrix.
- The quaternary Hadamard matrix of order 1910 is on the list of unknown orders of Zaitsev et al.'s page (last updated
  14 October 2026), and no other source constructs it.

**Credits.** As for [6796](../6796): the array goes back to Seberry–Whiteman and Szöllősi, and the complex
Yamada–Kiyasu template is the form of Zaitsev et al.'s matrices QH470 to QH1526. The character set T of the border is
that of Theorem 7 of Egan et al.; the character-bordered array is [TBORDERED.md](../../complex-hadamard/TBORDERED.md).
The sources searched are listed in [NOVELTY.md](../../NOVELTY.md).
