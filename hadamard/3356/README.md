# Hadamard matrix of order 3356

3356 = 4 · 839, with 839 prime.

## Construction

A bordered Goethals–Seidel array (Egan, Kotsireas, London and Ó Catháin, Theorem 7, with a subgroup of index 8) over
the group

```
G = ℤ₄ × ℤ₄ × ℤ₄ × ℤ₁₃,   |G| = 832 = 839 + 1 − 8,
```

with the subgroup K = 2ℤ₄ × 2ℤ₄ × 2ℤ₄ × ℤ₁₃ of index 8, so G/K ≅ ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ × ℤ₂. G does not split over K: every
element of order 2 lies in K. Four ±1 sequences a₀, …, a₃ on G satisfy

```
Σᵢ PAF_aᵢ(d) = −28   for d ∈ K, d ≠ 0,
Σᵢ PAF_aᵢ(d) =  +4   for d ∉ K.
```

The Goethals–Seidel array M developed over G has order 4 · 832 = 3328. It is bordered by 28 rows and columns:

```
H = [ X  R ]
    [ C  M ]
```

X is a Hadamard matrix of order 28, and the entries of R and C depend only on the coset of K. The conditions on the
border are those of [search/NOTES.md](../../search/NOTES.md) §18 with T the seven nontrivial characters of G/K. For
these sequences they depend only on the four values d below. The border was found by an exhaustive search over borders
with a cyclic symmetry of order 7, which permutes the seven nonzero cosets of K.

The sequences are constant on the orbits of a group S of order 21 acting on G. It is generated by

```
σ(x, z) = (Bx, 3z),   B = [[2, 2, 1], [1, 0, 0], [2, 3, 3]]   (mod 4, acting on columns x ∈ ℤ₄³).
```

B has order 7. Modulo 2 its characteristic polynomial is x³ + x² + 1, so it permutes the seven nonzero cosets of K
cyclically. ℤ₄³ is the additive group of the Galois ring GR(4, 3), and the subgroups of order 7 of GL(3, ℤ₄) are
conjugate, so ⟨B⟩ is conjugate to multiplication by the Teichmüller elements of order 7. 3 has order 3 modulo 13.
S has 50 orbits: sizes 1, 3 (4 orbits), 7 (9 orbits) and 21 (36 orbits). Since −1 ∉ S, the condition reduces to 25
equations.

| | a₀ | a₁ | a₂ | a₃ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| row sum | 20 | 30 | 30 | 34 |
| sum on K | 6 | 2 | 2 | 6 |
| sum on each of the seven other cosets of K | 2 | 4 | 4 | 4 |

The row sums satisfy 20² + 30² + 30² + 34² = 3356. The coset sums are aᵢ + dᵢ δ₀ with (aᵢ) = (2, 4, 4, 4) and
(dᵢ) = (4, −2, −2, 2), and Σ dᵢ² = 28 = 4(8 − 1).

The construction is that of [3964](../3964) with a subgroup of index 8 instead of 4. Alpöge et al.'s matrix of order
1436 = 4 · 359 also uses a subgroup of index 8 and a border of size 28. As in [2636](../2636) (ℤ₄ × ℤ₄ × ℤ₄₁ over
2ℤ₄ × 2ℤ₄ × ℤ₄₁), G does not split over K; here the 2-part is the Galois ring GR(4, 3), whose Teichmüller elements
permute all seven nonzero cosets.

## Search

**The structure.** It came from a census of Theorem 7 structures over groups whose 2-part is larger than G/K
([search/NOTES.md](../../search/NOTES.md) §102). For each of the eleven unresolved n with 8 | n + 1, the census took
every abelian 2-group with a subgroup of index 8, multiplier groups built from Teichmüller, Singer, Frobenius and
shear automorphisms, and units of the odd part. It ranked about 34,000 structures by a Gaussian lattice estimate and
tested the best ones by exact compression.

This structure predicts about 10^11.2 solutions. Two independent re-checks counted the compressed sequences exactly:

| Quotient | Exact count | Gaussian |
|---|---:|---:|
| G/ℤ₄³ = ℤ₁₃ | 10^40.71 | 10^42.48 |
| G/2ℤ₄³ = ℤ₂³ × ℤ₁₃ | 10^39.08 | 10^40.33 |
| G/ℤ₁₃ = ℤ₄³ | 10^39.2 (sampled) | 10^38.6 |

Every S-invariant quotient is non-empty. Corrected for the measured shortfall, the expectation is about 10^9.2
solutions, or 10^7 up to the symmetries. At n = 1031 (G of order 1024) the census found every structure with a
positive estimate exactly empty.

**The chain.** The search ([search/NOTES.md](../../search/NOTES.md) §102) is the compression chain of 3964,
generalized to any index and to quotients by any S-invariant subgroup.

1. **ℤ₁₃ level (N = ℤ₄³).** The lists have 13,930 / 7,852 / 7,852 / 13,741 compressed vectors. The join gives 80
   solutions, or 20 up to the normalizer of S, which has order 96 modulo S.
2. **ℤ₂³ × ℤ₁₃ level (N = 2ℤ₄³).** This gives 350 branches.
3. **ℤ₄³ level (N = ℤ₁₃).** It is restricted to the 2000 heaviest compressed vectors of each block, and the
   combinations are taken in random order.
   - An exact necessary condition modulo 8 rejects 91 % of them.
   - The rest are finished by an exact four-list join on all 25 equations, over fibres of about 1.5 · 10⁴
     sequences per block.

The restriction makes the search incomplete; it is not a classification. The first solution appeared in branch 72
after about 12 core-minutes, and five distinct solutions in about 90 core-minutes. The matrix here comes from the
first one.

## Status

**New.** The entry 839(4) is unresolved in Cati–Pasechnik Table 4, and Egan, Kotsireas, London and Ó Catháin list
4 · 839 among their open orders (Table 5, 19 October 2026). The order 8 · 839 = 6712 was settled in 1998 by Kamali and
Kharaghani (dihedral Golay sequences, Table 1), which both tables missed; the doubling of this matrix gives it again.

None of the standard recursive constructions applies:

- Paley: 3355 = 5 · 11 · 61 and 1677 = 3 · 13 · 43 are not prime powers.
- Miyamoto: 839 ≡ 3 (mod 4).
- Yamada: 837 = 3³ · 31 is not a prime power.
- Mathon: 1677 is not of the form q²(q + 2).

The sources searched are listed in [NOVELTY.md](../../NOVELTY.md).
