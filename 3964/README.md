# Order 3964

3964 = 4 · 991, with 991 prime.

## Construction

A bordered Goethals–Seidel array over the group

```
G = ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ × ℤ₂₄₇,   |G| = 988 = 991 + 1 − 4,
```

with the subgroup K = 0 × 0 × ℤ₂₄₇ of index 4. Four ±1 sequences a₀, …, a₃ on G
satisfy

```
Σᵢ PAF_aᵢ(d) = −12   for d ∈ K, d ≠ 0,
Σᵢ PAF_aᵢ(d) =  +4   for d ∉ K.
```

The Goethals–Seidel array M developed over G has order 4 · 988 = 3952. It is
bordered by twelve rows and columns:

```
H = [ X  R ]
    [ C  M ]
```

Here X is a 12 × 12 ±1 matrix, and the entries of R and C depend only on the coset
of K. With K = G and a border of size 4 this is Spence's construction. The general
conditions on the border are those of a small ±1 problem over G/K ≅ ℤ₂ × ℤ₂. For
these sequences a solution was found at once.

The sequences are constant on the orbits of a group S of order 27 acting on G. It
is generated by

```
σ(x, y) = (αx, 66y),   τ(x, y) = (x, 35y),
```

where α(x₁, x₂) = (x₂, x₁ + x₂) is an automorphism of order 3 of ℤ₂ × ℤ₂. S has
46 orbits, and the condition reduces to 23 equations.

| | a₀ | a₁ | a₂ | a₃ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| row sum | −50 | −26 | −2 | 28 |
| sums on the cosets (0,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,1) of K | −11, −13, −13, −13 | −5, −7, −7, −7 | 1, −1, −1, −1 | 7, 7, 7, 7 |

The row sums satisfy 50² + 26² + 2² + 28² = 3964. The structure is the one in
Alpöge et al.'s matrix of order 1916 = 4 · 479, carried over from ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ × ℤ₁₁₉
to ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ × ℤ₂₄₇ (see the [references](../README.md#references)).

## Search

The Gaussian model of [METHOD.md](../METHOD.md) predicts about 20 solutions in this
structure. That model is unreliable here: for the 1916 structure it predicted 7,
and the search below found more than 7800 in one branch. The search is exhaustive in
principle and works by compression.

For a subgroup N of G that is invariant under S, the sums of each sequence over
the cosets of N satisfy the same kind of equations on G/N.

1. **N of order 19.** All compressed vectors of each sequence were listed (3.2 to
   4.6 · 10⁵ per sequence) and joined. This gave 304 solutions.
2. **N of order 13.** Each solution of step 1 fixes a fibre of up to 10⁷ sequences
   per block. A dynamic program over the orbits lists only the 70 to 140 distinct
   compressed vectors in each fibre, which are joined again. Each solution of step 1
   that we looked at gave 160 consistent combinations.
3. **N = ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ × 0.** Each combination leaves 10⁴ to 5 · 10⁵ sequences per block.
   They were split once more by their sums over this subgroup and finished with
   an exact four-list join on all 23 equations.

Seven of the 304 branches were searched, each in its own process. Every one of them
gave solutions within its first three combinations, one to eight minutes after the
start. The matrix here comes from the first solution found.

## Status

New. The entry 991(3) is unresolved in Cati–Pasechnik Table 4 and does not appear
in Đoković's updates (arXiv:0912.5091, arXiv:1008.2043). None of the standard
recursive constructions apply:

- Paley: 3963 = 3 · 1321 and 1981 = 7 · 283 are not prime powers.
- Miyamoto: 991 ≡ 3 (mod 4).
- Yamada: 989 = 23 · 43 is not a prime power.
- Mathon: 1981 is not of the form q²(q + 2).
