# Hadamard matrix of order 12260

12260 = 4 · 3065, with 3065 = 5 · 613.

## Construction

Miyamoto's Hadamard matrix of order 2452 (q = 613, a prime ≡ 1 mod 4), built from Paley's conference matrix of order 614
and a Hadamard matrix of order 612 made of two negacyclic blocks, is a 4 × 4 array of bordered blocks of order 613 that
commute and are transposed by one permutation. Multiplying it by T-matrices of order 5 in the Goethals–Seidel array
gives the Hadamard matrix of order 4 · 613 · 5 = 12260. The construction and its proof are in
[MIYAMOTO_BLOCKS.md](../MIYAMOTO_BLOCKS.md).

- Paley's conference matrix of order 614, its points listed as ∞, 0, g^(2j), g^(2j+1) (j < 306) with the primitive root
  g = 2.
- The Hadamard matrix of order 612 is [[A, B], [−Bᵀ, Aᵀ]] with A, B negacyclic of order 306: a negaperiodic Golay pair
  of length 306, from symmetric circulant Williamson matrices of order 153 through the array (29) of Balonin–Đoković,
  read in ℤ[y]/(y^306 + 1). The Williamson matrices are constant on the orbits of the multiplier group generated by 145,
  35 and −1 on ℤ_153 and are found by the program in a second; Williamson(-type) matrices of order 153 = 17 · 18/2 are
  classical (J. Wallis 1973, Whiteman 1976; key w4 of Seberry–Yamada's Table A.1).
- T-matrices of order 5 (first rows, from base sequences BS(3, 2)): `++000`, `00+00`, `000+-`, `00000`.

## Search

None beyond the Williamson matrices: the matrix is determined by q, the pair and the T-matrices. `python3
search/miyamoto_blocks.py certify 613 5 wmult:153:145,35` writes the certificate.

## Status

**First explicit construction** ([MIYAMOTO_BLOCKS.md](../MIYAMOTO_BLOCKS.md#status)). Table 1.53 of the Handbook of
Combinatorial Designs (2nd ed., 2007) gives n = 3065 with t = 2, as known. The only construction we traced uses
Williamson-type matrices of order 613 from Seberry–Yamada's Table A.1 (1992). Table A.1 obtains Williamson-type matrices
of order 613 only from Corollary 8.8(i), whose printed proof does not give Williamson-type matrices
([NOTES.md](../../NOTES.md) §7). Table A.1 credits that case to Miyamoto (J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 57 (1991)), whose
paper was not accessible to us; we found no valid proof of it and no other construction of Williamson-type matrices of
order 613.

- Paley's constructions do not apply: 4n − 1 = 12259 = 13 · 23 · 41 and 2n − 1 = 6129 = 3³ · 227 are not prime powers.
- Miyamoto's theorem does not apply: it needs a symmetric conference matrix of order n + 1 = 3066, and none is known to
  us (3065 is not a prime power); Yamada–Kiyasu does not apply: n − 2 = 3063 = 3 · 1021 is not a prime power.
- Cooper–Wallis needs Williamson-type matrices of order 613 or T-matrices of order 613; neither is known to us.
- The Paley-block route does not reach it: 4s − 1 = 2451 and 12259 are not prime powers ≡ 3 (mod 8) for the other
  divisors s of 3065, and the remaining ones need T-matrices of order 3065 (s = 1) or 613 (s = 5), which are not known
  to us.
- SageMath's development version (17 October 2026) does not construct it: `hadamard_matrix(12260, existence=True)`
  raises UnknownError in its Miyamoto test (no symmetric conference matrix of order 3066 is known to it), and returns
  Unknown when that Unknown is treated as False.

We found no valid construction of order 12260 before this one. The sources searched are listed in
[NOVELTY.md](../../NOVELTY.md).
