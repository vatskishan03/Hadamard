# Hadamard matrix of order 26972

26972 = 4 · 6743, with 6743 = 11 · 613.

## Construction

Miyamoto's Hadamard matrix of order 2452 (q = 613, a prime ≡ 1 mod 4), built from Paley's conference matrix of order 614
and a Hadamard matrix of order 612 made of two negacyclic blocks, is a 4 × 4 array of bordered blocks of order 613 that
commute and are transposed by one permutation. Multiplying it by T-matrices of order 11 in the Goethals–Seidel array
gives the Hadamard matrix of order 4 · 613 · 11 = 26972. The construction and its proof are in
[MIYAMOTO_BLOCKS.md](../MIYAMOTO_BLOCKS.md).

- Paley's conference matrix of order 614, its points listed as ∞, 0, g^(2j), g^(2j+1) (j < 306) with the primitive root
  g = 2.
- The Hadamard matrix of order 612 is [[A, B], [−Bᵀ, Aᵀ]] with A, B negacyclic of order 306: a negaperiodic Golay pair
  of length 306, from symmetric circulant Williamson matrices of order 153 through the array (29) of Balonin–Đoković,
  read in ℤ[y]/(y^306 + 1). The Williamson matrices are constant on the orbits of the multiplier group generated by 145,
  35 and −1 on ℤ_153 and are found by the program in a second; Williamson(-type) matrices of order 153 = 17 · 18/2 are
  classical (J. Wallis 1973, Whiteman 1976; key w4 of Seberry–Yamada's Table A.1).
- T-matrices of order 11 (first rows, from base sequences BS(6, 5)): `++00+000000`, `00++0-00000`, `000000++-+-`,
  `00000000000`.

## Search

None beyond the Williamson matrices: the matrix is determined by q, the pair and the T-matrices. `python3
search/miyamoto_blocks.py certify 613 11 wmult:153:145,35` writes the certificate.

## Status

**First explicit construction** ([MIYAMOTO_BLOCKS.md](../MIYAMOTO_BLOCKS.md#status)). Table 1.53 of the Handbook of
Combinatorial Designs (2nd ed., 2007) gives n = 6743 with t = 3, and Đoković (arXiv:0912.5091) lists it in his set Δ of
entries that should have had t = 2. His proof for Δ takes Williamson-type matrices from Seberry–Yamada's Table A.1
(1992); for 6743 they are of order 613. Table A.1 obtains Williamson-type matrices of order 613 only from Corollary
8.8(i), whose printed proof does not give Williamson-type matrices ([NOTES.md](../../NOTES.md) §7). Table A.1 credits
that case to Miyamoto (J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 57 (1991)), whose paper was not accessible to us; we found no valid
proof of it and no other construction of Williamson-type matrices of order 613.

- Paley's constructions do not apply: 4n − 1 = 26971 = 7 · 3853 and 2n − 1 = 13485 = 3 · 5 · 29 · 31 are not prime
  powers.
- Miyamoto's theorem does not apply: it needs a symmetric conference matrix of order n + 1, which cannot exist for n ≡ 3
  (mod 4); Yamada–Kiyasu does not apply: n − 2 = 6741 = 3² · 7 · 107 is not a prime power.
- Cooper–Wallis needs Williamson-type matrices of order 613 or T-matrices of order 613; neither is known to us.
- The Paley-block route does not reach it: 4s − 1 = 2451 and 26971 are not prime powers ≡ 3 (mod 8) for the other
  divisors s of 6743, and the remaining ones need T-matrices of order 6743 (s = 1) or 613 (s = 11), which are not known
  to us.
- SageMath's development version (17 October 2026) does not construct it: `hadamard_matrix(26972, existence=True)`
  returns Unknown.

We found no valid construction of order 26972 before this one. The sources searched are listed in
[NOVELTY.md](../../NOVELTY.md).
